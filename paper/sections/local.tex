\section{The four-term identity}\label{sec:local}

This section contains the only local identity used in the proof, together with
two facts about highest reachable vertices.
We state them for sets of edges rather than orientations, since later we
apply them to sets obtained by adding or deleting single edges.

Let $\eta$ be a set of pairs $uv$ with $u<v$ in $[n]$, regarded as edges directed
upwards, or a translation-invariant set of such pairs of integers whose
projection to $\setZ/n\setZ$ is acyclic.
In both cases every vertex $z$ has a highest reachable vertex $\hrv{\eta}{z}$;
in the periodic case, the bound follows as in Section~\ref{subs:circularDefinitions}.
The map $\hrv{\eta}{\cdot}$ is weakly decreasing along every edge of $\eta$, and
$\hrv{\eta}{z}$ is the largest sink, that is, vertex without outgoing edges,
which can be reached from $z$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ineffective}
Let $\eta$ be as above.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Let $\xi$ be a set of upward pairs, translation invariant in the periodic
case, such that $\hrv{\eta}{y}\leq\hrv{\eta}{x}$ for every $xy\in\xi$.
Then $\hrv{\eta\cup\xi}{\cdot}=\hrv{\eta}{\cdot}$, and in the periodic case
$\eta\cup\xi$ still has acyclic projection.
\item If $\eta$ contains a path from $x$ to $y$ avoiding the edge $xy$, then
deleting $xy$ does not change reachability.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For (i), $\hrv{\eta}{\cdot}$ is weakly decreasing along every edge of
$\eta\cup\xi$.
Hence every vertex reachable from $z$ in $\eta\cup\xi$ is at most
$\hrv{\eta}{z}$, and the maximizing path in $\eta$ is still available.
In the periodic case, a directed cycle in the projection would lift to a path
from $z$ to $z+kn$ for some $k\geq1$, and then
$\hrv{\eta}{z}+kn=\hrv{\eta}{z+kn}\leq\hrv{\eta}{z}$, which is absurd.
For (ii), every path using $xy$ can be rerouted along the alternative path.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sinkRelabel}
Let $\eta$ and $\eta'$ be sets of upward pairs in $[n]$, and let $\sigma$ be a
permutation of $[n]$ mapping $\eta$ bijectively onto $\eta'$.
If $\sigma$ is increasing on the sinks of $\eta$, then
$\hrv{\eta'}{\sigma(z)}=\sigma(\hrv{\eta}{z})$ for all $z$.
In particular the fibres of $\hrv{\eta'}{\cdot}$ are the images of the fibres of
$\hrv{\eta}{\cdot}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The vertex $\hrv{\eta}{z}$ is the largest sink of $\eta$ reachable from $z$,
and $\sigma$ maps the sinks of $\eta$ reachable from $z$ onto the sinks of
$\eta'$ reachable from $\sigma(z)$, preserving their order.
\end{proof}

We use the Iverson bracket: $[A]=1$ when the statement $A$ holds, and $[A]=0$
otherwise.

\begin{lemma}[The four-term identity]\label{lem:fourTerm}
Let $\eta$ be a set of upward pairs in $[n]$.
Let $u<v<w$ be vertices such that $uw\in\eta$, and $uv,vw\notin\eta$.
Put $\eta_{00}=\eta$, $\eta_{10}=\eta+uv$, $\eta_{01}=\eta+vw$ and
$\eta_{11}=\eta+uv+vw$, and write $\Kp(\eta_{ij})$ for $\Kp$ of the transitive
closure.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item For every coloring $\kappa:[n]\to\setNN$ which increases along the edges
of $\eta$,
\[
 [\kappa \text{ increases on } \eta_{10}]+[\kappa \text{ increases on } \eta_{01}]
 =1+[\kappa \text{ increases on } \eta_{11}].
\]
In particular $\Kp(\eta_{10})+\Kp(\eta_{01})=\Kp(\eta_{00})+\Kp(\eta_{11})$,
and the same holds for the sums over colorings with prescribed colors at any set
of vertices.
\item The four sets can be paired, either as $\{\eta_{00},\eta_{01}\}$ and
$\{\eta_{10},\eta_{11}\}$ or as $\{\eta_{00},\eta_{10}\}$ and
$\{\eta_{01},\eta_{11}\}$, so that paired sets have the same highest reachable
vertices.
In particular $\Phi(\eta_{10})+\Phi(\eta_{01})=\Phi(\eta_{00})+\Phi(\eta_{11})$
for every function $\Phi$ of the highest reachable vertex map, for example
$\Phi=\elementaryE_{\lambda}$ of its fibre sizes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

The lemma has no cover hypotheses, and the other edges of $\eta$ are arbitrary.
Writing each set for its value, the identity reads
\[
\FTpic{1}{0}\;+\;\FTpic{0}{1}\;=\;\FTpic{0}{0}\;+\;\FTpic{1}{1}\,,
\]
where the dotted line stands for the other vertices and edges, which are the
same in all four graphs.

\begin{proof}
For (i), write $a,b,c$ for the colors at $u,v,w$, so that $a<c$.
Then $[a<b]+[b<c]=1+[a<b<c]$, which is the displayed identity.
Summing $x_\kappa$ over all $\kappa$ increasing on $\eta$, with any colors
prescribed, gives the second statement.

For (ii), write $h(z)=\hrv{\eta}{z}$.
Since $uw\in\eta$, $h(w)\leq h(u)$.
Suppose first that $h(w)\leq h(v)$.
Adding $uv$ does not change the highest reachable vertices of $v$ and $w$, since
no path starting at $v$ or $w$ uses an edge leaving $u<v$.
Hence $\hrv{\eta_{10}}{w}\leq\hrv{\eta_{10}}{v}$ as well, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:ineffective}(i) for $\xi=\{vw\}$ pairs $\eta_{00}$ with $\eta_{01}$
and $\eta_{10}$ with $\eta_{11}$.
Suppose instead that $h(v)<h(w)$.
Then $h(v)<h(w)\leq h(u)$.
In $\eta_{01}$, a path from $v$ either avoids $vw$ or continues from $w$, so
$\hrv{\eta_{01}}{v}\leq\max(h(v),h(w))=h(w)\leq\hrv{\eta_{01}}{u}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:ineffective}(i) for $\xi=\{uv\}$, applied to $\eta$ and to
$\eta_{01}$, gives the second pairing.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:fourTerm} does not assert that $\eta_{10}$ and $\eta_{01}$ have
the same highest reachable vertex map.
In the first case of the proof, $\eta_{10}$ has the same highest reachable
vertices as $\eta_{11}$, not as $\eta_{01}$.
This is why the identity is used for sums over many orientations at once.
