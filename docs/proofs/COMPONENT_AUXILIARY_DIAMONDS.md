# Auxiliary diamonds and the remaining component step

2026-09-28. Research toward the user's requested accessible diamond proof.
The component theorem is already proved and formalized by the finite word
argument. This note records a different, incomplete route. The diamond
quotient and relabeling lemmas below have mathematical proofs; the proposed
universal identity has only bounded exact evidence. No manuscript proof or
Lean theorem has been replaced.

We allow auxiliary posets, including posets which omit original strict edges.
The [earlier obstruction and repair](DIAMOND_STRAIGHTENING_AUDIT.md) explain
why this is necessary. We use the strict-coloring/elementary convention here.

## All auxiliary diamonds admit an explicit expansion

Let A_n be the free abelian group on naturally labeled posets on [n]. Its
multiplication is union followed by transitive closure. All relations point
from a smaller label to a larger label, so the union remains acyclic. Write
x_ij for the poset with its single relation i<j. These commuting generators
are idempotent, and transitivity gives, for i<j<k,

    x_ij x_jk (1-x_ik) = 0.

Let D_n be the quotient by all auxiliary diamond relations, with arbitrary
forward exterior relations. Equivalently we also impose

    x_ik (1-x_ij)(1-x_jk) = 0.                         (1)

We claim that the map

    [P] ↦ (1_{π is a linear extension of P})_(π∈S_n)

identifies D_n with the ring Z^(S_n), with coordinatewise multiplication.
In particular, this assertion is integral, not just a dimension calculation
over the rationals.

To prove it, expand the identity

    1 = ∏_(i<j) (x_ij + (1-x_ij)).

A term chooses one direction for every pair, hence a tournament. A cyclic
tournament has a directed triangle. Its factor is either the transitivity
relation above or (1), so that term vanishes. The surviving tournaments are
the total orders, indexed by permutations. Denote their terms by ε_π. They
are orthogonal idempotents whose evaluations at permutations are the standard
coordinate vectors. Thus they span freely, and

    [P] = Σ_(π∈LE(P)) ε_π.                            (2)

An especially useful expression needs only adjacent positions:

    ε_π = ∏_(π_i<π_(i+1)) x_(π_i,π_(i+1))
          ∏_(π_i>π_(i+1)) (1-x_(π_(i+1),π_i)).        (3)

Indeed, these adjacent comparisons uniquely specify the total order π;
the coordinate description proves the equality in D_n. Expanding (3)
expresses ε_π as a signed sum of forward posets admitting generating
relations whose undirected edges lie on a path.

Both the coloring enumerator and the HRV elementary expression satisfy the
diamond relation, so both are additive maps out of D_n. We do not assert
that either valuation respects union multiplication. Equation (2) gives a
diamond proof of the refinement previously obtained using mixed posets in
[the permutation formula](COMPONENT_MIXED_POSET.md).

## A terminating diamond reduction to forests

There is also a direct reduction with no signed permutation notation. Every
forward poset is an integral combination, using only diamonds, of increasing
forests with at most one incoming cover at each vertex.

Choose the greatest vertex v with two cover predecessors a<b. Let P_0 be
the transitive closure of the Hasse graph of P after deleting b→v. The
selected long edge a→v and its two short edges a→b, b→v give

    [P] = [P_0] + [P+ab] - [P_0+ab].

The first and third terms lose the incoming cover b→v. In the second term,
a→v becomes redundant through a→b→v. No new cover can appear except a→b:
each resulting order is generated by a subset of the original Hasse edges
and this one additional edge. Consequently each term strictly decreases
the vector of incoming Hasse degrees at n,n-1,...,1 lexicographically.
This is a bounded vector of nonnegative integers, so the reduction terminates.

It matters that we delete an edge from the Hasse generating graph. Deleting
only the comparison b<v from the full transitive relation could leave
previously redundant comparisons which become new covers.

There are exactly n! such increasing forests: each vertex v chooses no
parent or one parent in {1,...,v-1}. They span the free group D_n of rank n!,
so they form an integral basis. The remaining obstruction is outgoing
branching, not arbitrary poset structure. Individual branching forests
need not have symmetric coloring enumerators and cannot all reduce to
chain posets under identities preserving that enumerator.

## Relabelings which preserve the two valuations

A permutation σ of the labels is allowed on P if σP remains naturally
labeled and σ preserves the relative order of the sinks of P. Relabeling
colors preserves the coloring enumerator. Every greatest reachable vertex
is a sink; preservation of sink order therefore conjugates the HRV map and
preserves its fiber sizes. We may consequently impose [P]=[σP] additively.

These relabeling relations do **not** automatically form an ideal under union.
Adjoining an exterior E requires comparing P∪E with σP∪σE, not σP∪E.
Every use of a relabeling below checks the entire poset.

Here is one useful consequence. Let σ exchange consecutive labels a,a+1.
Suppose their positions in π are nonadjacent and a label greater than a+1
occurs to the right of at least one of them. Then

    ε_π = ε_(σπ).                                    (4)

For a proof, specify every pair comparison of π except the pair {a,a+1}.
Nonadjacency of their positions ensures that the omitted comparison follows
from the others. This Boolean product still represents ε_π in D_n. The
assumed larger label supplies a mandatory outgoing edge from at least one
of a,a+1 in every term of its signed expansion. No term contains the edge
a→a+1. Thus σ preserves forwardness in every term, and the two exchanged
vertices are never both sinks. Since their labels are consecutive, σ
preserves the relative order of all sinks. Apply the allowed relabeling to
each whole term. The resulting Boolean product represents ε_(σπ).

This argument does not multiply a previously imposed relabeling relation
by a new exterior. It pairs fully specified auxiliary posets.

There is also a useful normalization. Suppose π and τ put n in the same
position, have the same descent set, and have order-isomorphic suffixes
after n. Then (4) implies ε_π=ε_τ. To see this, regard labelings of the
positions other than n as linear extensions of the following position
poset: retain the prescribed adjacent comparisons in the prefix, and retain
all comparisons in the suffix. Any two such labelings are connected by
exchanging consecutive labels at incomparable positions. Those positions
are nonadjacent and are not both in the suffix. At least one precedes n,
so n is the outgoing witness required in (4).

In particular, the prefix may be assigned the smallest available labels,
with any fixed arrangement having its prescribed descent set. This reduces
the number of cases but does not by itself produce chains.

## A precise sufficient identity, still unproved

Further impose additive identities between all disjoint unions of chains
with the same chain-size partition; both valuations take the value e_λ
on such a poset. Call the resulting additive quotient Q_n. For a poset P,
let C_(λ(P)) be any such chain poset with the HRV fiber-size partition of P.

A sufficient universal statement would be the existence of elements
T_(ρ;a,b,c;σ) of Q_n, independent of P, such that

    [P] - [C_(λ(P))]
      = Σ_(ρ,a<b<c,σ)
        (1_(ρacbσ∈LE(P)) + 1_(ρbcaσ∈LE(P))
         - 1_(ρcabσ∈LE(P)) - 1_(ρbacσ∈LE(P)))
        T_(ρ;a,b,c;σ).                               (5)

The prefix and suffix use each remaining label exactly once. Summing (5)
over a complete ordinary component would finish the proof: the actual flip
bijection gives the four-term relation for its linear-extension counts,
and hence every coefficient of T vanishes. The component label is retained
throughout. This uses the already proved local flip-count relation, not
the completed component expansion.

A rational version of (5), in Q_n tensored with Q, would already suffice:
both valuations take values in torsion-free polynomial rings. Integral
coefficients would strengthen the reduction but are not required for the
component formula.

In degree three, (5) follows directly from the triangle diamond and the
allowed chain identities. The element T can be the outgoing fork minus a
chain of length two and a singleton. The incoming-fork defect is its
negative. In higher degree we do not yet have a uniform construction of T
or an induction proving (5).

The four-term word relation belongs to the coefficient side of (5).
It must not be imposed directly on the ε terms: already in degree three,
ε_132+ε_231-ε_312-ε_213 has a nonzero coloring enumerator. Also, the
existing scalar word/Cauchy identity does not establish its lift to Q_n.

## Exact finite checks and their limits

We performed exact rational row reduction, eliminating all diamonds first
by (2). The remaining rows consist only of whole-poset relabelings preserving
sink order, and equal-size chain identities. Relabeling rows were never
multiplied by a fixed exterior.

| n | Forward posets | dim Q_n over Q | Ordinary components checked | (5) over Q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | passes |
| 3 | 7 | 4 | 16 | passes |
| 4 | 40 | 10 | 132 | passes |
| 5 | 357 | 25 | 1559 | passes |

The component check sums [P]-[C_(λ(P))] over each actual complete component
of every undecorated ordinary area sequence. Strict corner decorations need
no separate enumeration: the established undecorated-component lemma says
they retain whole components, since a corner cannot be a short flip edge.

For the stronger test of (5), expand ε_π using (3) and replace each poset
in that signed expansion by its HRV chain poset. Subtract this result from
ε_π, then reduce in Q_n. For every surviving coordinate, its row indexed by
π lies in the span of the contextual four-word rows. This is exactly the
finite-dimensional factorization required by (5) over Q. It does not prove
integrality or a statement for arbitrary n.

A further test rules out a particularly simple normal-form strategy. There
are 12 distinct classes of individual ε_π in Q_4, although its dimension
is 10. In degree five there are 32 classes and dimension 25. Thus genuine
relations among several terms remain; pairwise identification of permutation
terms cannot describe the whole quotient.
